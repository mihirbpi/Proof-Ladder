Here is a fact that looks like magic and is not.

> Add up odd numbers in order, and you always land on a square number.

$1 = 1$. And $1+3 = 4$. And $1+3+5 = 9$. And $1+3+5+7 = 16$.

$1, 4, 9, 16$ — and those are exactly the squares you can build with tiles: $1$-by-$1$, $2$-by-$2$, $3$-by-$3$, $4$-by-$4$.

## Why, with tiles

Build it and the reason becomes obvious.

Start with $1$ tile. That is a $1$-by-$1$ square.

To make it a $2$-by-$2$ square, what do you need? A tile on the right, a tile on top, and one in the corner. **3 tiles**, in an L-shape.

To make *that* a $3$-by-$3$ square, you need $2$ down the side, $2$ across the top, and $1$ in the corner. **5 tiles**.

Next shell: $3 + 3 + 1 = $ **7 tiles**.

Then $4+4+1 = $ **9 tiles**.

## The odd numbers appear on their own

Look at the shell sizes: $3$, $5$, $7$, $9$. All odd!

And there is a reason. Every **shell** is *some number down the side*, plus *the same number across the top*, plus *one for the corner*.

Two equal piles, plus one extra. That is exactly what an odd number is (module 4.2)!

So the shells *have* to be odd. They could not be anything else.

## The domino argument

<Proof level="l2">
**Push the first one.** One tile is a square. ✓

**Check the chain.** Suppose I have a square built out of shells so far.

Adding the next odd number means adding the next shell. And a square plus its next shell is the next square up.

So if I had a square, I still have a square. ✓

Both jobs done. So adding up odd numbers always gives a square, forever.
</Proof>

<Aside>
Notice that the picture and the domino argument are the *same* proof. The picture shows what each shell does; the dominoes explain why that keeps working forever. You can trust either one, and it is nice to have both.
</Aside>
