The domino trick is powerful, but it does not work on everything. Here is how to tell.

## Claims that come one after another

> Every whole number, doubled, is even.

Check the shape of this. There is a case for $1$, a case for $2$, a case for $3$, and so on. They come **one after another**, and you can count them.

Dominoes work here.

> Adding up odd numbers gives a square number.

Same shape. A case for the first odd number, then the first two, then the first three. One after another. Dominoes work.

## Claims that are not in a line

> Every number on the number line, times itself, is zero or bigger.

Now try to line these up. What is the first case? Is it $0$? Then what comes next? Whatever tiny number you pick, half of it is smaller and also needs checking. And half of *that*. And smaller still, forever.

**There is no next one.** Between any two numbers on the line there is always another (module 4.6).

No line, no dominoes.

(That claim is true, by the way. But you prove it a different way — take any one and follow it, as in module 1.12.)

## Two questions to ask

Before reaching for dominoes, ask:

1. **Can I count the cases?** First, second, third... If not, dominoes are the wrong tool.
2. **Does each case help with the next?** If knowing case $5$ tells you nothing at all about case $6$, the chain has a gap.

Both must be yes.

<Warning title="Dominoes are one tool, not the only tool">
If dominoes do not apply, that does not mean the claim is false. It means you need a different method.

Module 1.12 gave you *take any one and follow it*, which works when the cases do not need to be lined up. Chapter 2 gave you *check both boxes*. Module 2.5 gave you *suppose not*. Choosing the right one is a real skill.
</Warning>

<Aside>
Question 2 catches people out more often than question 1. You can have perfectly countable cases with no useful link between them. If case $k+1$ has nothing to do with case $k$, then even after assuming case $k$ you are no closer, and the inductive step will not go through.
</Aside>

Say a claim about "every whole number." Then say one about "every number on the number line." Which one comes in a line?
