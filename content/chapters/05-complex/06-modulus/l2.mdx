Every complex number sits at a spot on the grid. How far is that spot from the middle?

<Figure alt="A grid with an arrow from the centre to a point three across and four up." caption="Three across, four up. How long is the arrow?">

</Figure>

## Make a triangle

Look at the picture. The arrow, the across walk, and the up walk make a **right triangle**.

The two short sides are $3$ and $4$. The long slanted side is the one we want.

Here is a rule about right triangles that people have known for thousands of years:

> Square the two short sides, add them up, and you get the square of the long side.

So: a $3$-by-$3$ square holds $9$. A $4$-by-$4$ square holds $16$. Add them: $9 + 16 = 25$.

That is the *square* of the long side. So the long side is the number that gives $25$ when you square it.

A $5$-by-$5$ square holds $25$. So the **distance** is $5$!

## Try another one

Where is $6 + 8i$?

Short sides $6$ and $8$. Squares: $36$ and $64$. Add: $100$.

What squares to $100$? $10$. So the distance is $10$.

## The nice surprise

Remember module 5.4, where you joined a number to its flip?

*Remember the little dash from module 5.3: $3-4i$ means three across and four the **other** way.*

$(3+4i)(3-4i) = 9 + 16 = 25$.

That is the same $25$! Multiplying a number by its own flip gives you the *square of the distance*.

Two things that looked completely different turn out to be the same thing.

<Aside>
For a spot sitting right on the flat line, the distance from the middle is just how far along it is. The spot at $3$ is $3$ away. So this new measuring stick agrees with the one you already had.
</Aside>
