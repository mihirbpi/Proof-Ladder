Multiplying by $i$ turns you a quarter turn. So what happens if you keep going?

Start at $1$ — facing right.

Multiply by $i$: you get $i$. Facing up.

Multiply by $i$ again. Now you are facing **left** — the opposite of where you began.

Again: facing **down**.

Again: facing **right**. **Home!**

## The pattern

> **up**, then **left**, then **down**, then **right** — and then up again, forever.

Four steps and it repeats. Forever. We call it **the cycle**.

It never gets more complicated than those four directions. No matter how many times you multiply by $i$, you are always facing one of those four ways.

## Which one will you land on?

Just count around. Multiply by $i$ seven times?

Seven is four and three more. The four take you all the way round, so they do not count. You only really do three: up, then left, then **down**.

So you end up facing down.

## Why grown-ups care

There is a famous formula in mathematics with $i$ in it, and it connects turning to a completely different part of mathematics.

You will not meet the formula properly for years. But when you do, the thing that makes it work is exactly **the cycle** you just found — four steps and back to the start.

Sometimes the small idea a young person can hold in their hand is the same idea doing the heavy lifting in a formula on a university blackboard.

<Aside>
The formula is $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$, and people call it one of the most beautiful in mathematics. Every symbol in it is something you can meet later. The cycle is what splits it into two halves.
</Aside>
