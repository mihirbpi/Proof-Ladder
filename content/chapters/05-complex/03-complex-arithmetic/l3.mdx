You have a new number $i$ that turns you a quarter turn, and numbers written as an across part plus a turn part. Now: how do you calculate with them?

The answer is reassuring. **You already know how.** Use every rule you already have, and whenever $i \times i$ appears, replace it with $-1$.

## First, numbers below zero

That $-1$ needs a word, because you meet these numbers properly next year.

Picture the number line. Walk right from $0$ and you pass $1, 2, 3$. Now walk the other way instead — past $0$, and keep going. Those spots need names too, so they take the same names with a small dash in front: $-1$, $-2$, $-3$. Say $-1$ as **"negative one."** It sits exactly one step from $0$, on the backwards side.

One fact is all this chapter needs from them: **the numbers with a dash are the backwards ones.** So "turning you backwards", from module 5.1, and "landing on $-1$" are the same thing.

## Adding

Keep the parts separate and add them. Add the across parts together, add the turn parts together, and do not mix them up.

Example: $(3 + 2i) + (1 + 5i) = 4 + 7i$.

## Multiplying by an ordinary number

$$
5 \times (2 + 3i) = 10 + 15i.
$$
Just multiply both parts. On the grid, this stretches your walk by a factor of $5$ — same direction, five times as far.

## Multiplying two complex numbers

Multiply out the brackets the same way you would multiply two two-digit numbers — every part of the first times every part of the second — then use $i \times i = -1$.

$$
(2 + 3i)(4 + 5i)
$$

Expand every pair:
$$
= 2\times4 \;+\; 2\times 5i \;+\; 3i \times 4 \;+\; 3i \times 5i
$$
$$
= 8 + 10i + 12i + 15(i \times i).
$$

Now the key step. $i \times i = -1$, so $15(i\times i) = -15$.

$$
= 8 + 10i + 12i - 15 = (8 - 15) + (10+12)i = -7 + 22i.
$$

That is the whole method. Expand, replace $i\times i$ by $-1$, collect the parts.

## The most important multiplication

What happens when you multiply by $i$ itself?

Start at $1$ — that is one step across, no step up. Multiply by $i$:
$$
1 \times i = i,
$$
which is *no* steps across and *one* step up. You were pointing across; now you are pointing up. **A quarter turn.**

Do it again:
$$
i \times i = -1,
$$
which is one step backwards. Another quarter turn — now you are pointing the opposite way.

Two quarter turns make a half turn, and a half turn is exactly what "multiply by $-1$" does. So $i \times i = -1$ is not a strange rule imposed from outside. **It is what a quarter turn has to be**, if doing it twice is to reverse you.

<Warning title="Watch the sign">
The most common slip is forgetting that $i \times i$ is $-1$ rather than $1$. When $3i$ meets $5i$ you get $15 \times (-1) = -15$, and that minus sign is easy to lose.

Whenever your answer looks too big and too positive, check whether you dropped a sign there.
</Warning>
