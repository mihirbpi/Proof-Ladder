A complex number is a move: so far across, so far up. To add two of them, you **combine the moves** — do one, then the other.

## On the grid

Start at the corner. Do the move $3 + 2i$: three across, two up. Mark the spot.

Now, from there, do the move $1 + 4i$: one more across, four more up.

You have gone $3 + 1 = 4$ across altogether, and $2 + 4 = 6$ up altogether.

$$(3 + 2i) + (1 + 4i) = 4 + 6i.$$

## The rule

Add the across parts. Add the up parts. Keep them separate.

$$(a + bi) + (c + di) = (a+c) + (b+d)i.$$

That is all there is to adding.

## Some to try

$(1 + 1i) + (2 + 3i) = 3 + 4i$.

$(5 + 0i) + (0 + 2i) = 5 + 2i$. (Notice: an ordinary number plus a straight-up one.)

$(3 + 2i) + (-3 - 2i) = 0 + 0i = 0$. You walked out and walked straight back!

## Do not mix the parts

Here is the one mistake to avoid.

$$(3 + 2i) + (1 + 4i) \quad\text{is NOT}\quad 3 + 2 + 1 + 4 = 10.$$

Across parts and up parts are different kinds of thing. You would not add "3 apples and 2 oranges" into "5 applesoranges." Keep them in their own columns.

<Aside>
Multiplying is more interesting, and you will meet it later. Here is a taste: multiplying by $i$ does not stretch anything — it *turns* you a quarter turn. Do it twice and you are facing backwards, which is the whole idea behind $i$.
</Aside>
