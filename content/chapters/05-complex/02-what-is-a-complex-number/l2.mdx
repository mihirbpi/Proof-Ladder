A complex number is a pair of instructions: **how far across**, and **how far up**.

We write it as $a + bi$. The $a$ is the **across part**. The $b$ is the **up part**.

$3 + 4i$ means: go $3$ across, then $4$ up.

$5 + 0i$ means: go $5$ across, and no up at all. That is just $5$.

$0 + 2i$ means: no across, just $2$ up.

## Ordinary numbers are in there

Look again at $5 + 0i$. It is a complex number *and* it is the ordinary number $5$.

So every ordinary number is already a complex number — the kind with no up part. Nothing was lost when we made the new numbers. Everything old is still here.

## Boxes inside boxes

You have now met four kinds of number, and each kind sits inside the next.

- **Counting numbers.** $0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ Example: $7$.
- **Pieces.** Adds halves, thirds, quarters. Example: half a cookie.
- **All the lengths on the ruler.** Adds the slanted line from module 4.7.
- **Complex numbers.** Adds the up part.

Draw it as four boxes, each inside the next. The counting numbers sit in the smallest box, right in the middle. Complex numbers are the biggest box of all.

Every number you have ever met is somewhere in that picture.

<Aside>
Watch out for one thing. In $3 + 4i$, the up part is **4**, not $4i$. The $i$ is not part of the number — it is the label saying "this bit is the up bit." It is like writing "$3$ apples": the number is $3$, and *apples* tells you what kind.
</Aside>
