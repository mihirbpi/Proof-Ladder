You can glue two tellings together with **and** or with **or**. The glue changes everything.

## And

*It is raining **and** it is cold.*

To say yes to this, you need both. Rain by itself? Not enough. Cold by itself? Not enough. You need rain *and* cold, together, on the same day.

So an **and** is easy to knock down. Find one part that is false and you are finished — you do not even have to look at the other part. A cold, sunny day breaks it. A warm, rainy day breaks it too.

## Or

*It is raining **or** it is cold.*

Now you only need one of them. Rainy? Yes. Cold? Yes. Rainy and cold at the same time?

Also yes! This is the tricky part. In math, **or** does not mean "pick one." It means "at least one." Both is always allowed.

An **or** is hard to knock down. To say no, you have to shoot down *every* part. One sunny warm day does it — no rain, no cold, nothing left.

<Warning title="Not like at dinner">
When a grown-up says "you can have soup or salad," they mean pick one. Math is more generous. Math **or** means at least one, and both is fine.
</Warning>

## Two funny tellings

*The door is open and the door is not open.*

That one is **never true**. Not today, not ever. It fights itself.

*The door is open or the door is not open.*

That one is **never false**. It has to be one or the other. You could not break it if you tried.

Those two tellings are made of the same pieces. The glue is the only difference, and the glue decided everything.

Say a telling that is never true. Then say one that is never false. Then say one that is true today but might be false tomorrow.
</TryIt>
