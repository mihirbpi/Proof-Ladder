Here is the whole chart. Four boxes. Learn it once and you have it forever.

|  | Show it is **true** | Show it is **false** |
|---|---|---|
| **Every** one works | Take any one and follow it | Find one bad apple |
| **Some** one works | Find one that works | Check every single one |

Notice how it goes diagonally. Where one job is easy, the other is hard.

## Take any one and follow it

This is the new move, and it is the clever one.

> **Every** number you can count to, doubled, is even.

You cannot check every number. There are too many! They never stop.

So instead: **take any one and follow it.** Do not pick a favorite. Pick a number you know nothing about, and call it "my number."

<Proof level="l2">
Take any counting number. Call it my number.

Now double it. That means you have two copies of my number, side by side.

Two equal piles! So it can be shared fairly between two people.

That is what even means. So double my number is even.

And we never said which number it was. So this works for all of them.
</Proof>

That is the trick. Because you never used anything special about your number, your reason works for every number at once.

## The other three

- **One bad apple** (module 1.11): to break an *every* claim, one is enough.
- **Find one** : to build a *some* claim, one is enough.
- **Check them all**: to break a *some* claim, nothing less will do.

<TryIt>
Say: *every crayon in this box has a point.* Which job is it? Then say: *some crayon in this box is blue.* Which job now? Do the easy one of each.
</TryIt>
