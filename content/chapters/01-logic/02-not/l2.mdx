Every telling has an **opposite**. The opposite of *the door is open* is *the door is not open*.

If one is true, the other is false. If one is false, the other is true. They can never both be true, and they can never both be false.

## Careful with "opposite"

Here is a trap. Suppose all your blocks are red.

*All the blocks are red.* — True.

What is the opposite? It is **not** *all the blocks are blue*. That is a different telling, and it can also be false.

The real opposite is: *not all the blocks are red* — meaning at least one of the blocks is not red. If even one block is a different color, the first telling was already broken.

<Warning title="Watch out">
The opposite of *all of them* is not *none of them*. The opposite of *all of them are red* is *at least one is not red*. One is enough to break the rule.
</Warning>

<TryIt>
Say the sentence *all the blocks are red*. Then say two different sentences. One should be the true opposite. One should just be a different sentence that isn't the opposite. See if a grown-up can tell which is which.
</TryIt>
