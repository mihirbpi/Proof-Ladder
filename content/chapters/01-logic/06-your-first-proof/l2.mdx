Being right is good. Showing *why* you are right is better. That is called a **reason chain**, and here is how you build one.

## Sam's apples

Sam has 4 apples. Sam's grandma hands over 2 more. How many apples does Sam have?

You already know the answer is 6. But watch how we say the *why*.

<Discussion level="l2">
  <div slot="know">Sam has 4 apples. Grandma gives Sam 2 more apples.</div>
  <div slot="want">We want to know how many apples Sam has now.</div>
  <div slot="how">Start from what you're given — the 4 apples — and add on the new ones.</div>
</Discussion>

<Proof level="l2">
Sam starts with 4 apples. Grandma gives Sam 2 more. So Sam has 4 + 2 apples. And 4 + 2 = 6. So Sam has 6 apples.
</Proof>

Every sentence in that chain leans on the one before it. That is why it is a **chain**. Pull out one link and the whole thing falls apart.

## The little box at the end

Did you see the small square at the end? □

That is how math people say "done, and I am sure." It means the chain reached the end and nothing is missing.

## Start from what you're given

That is the name of the move, and you will use it forever. You do not start from the answer. You start from the stuff you already have, and you walk.

Sam has 3 stickers and buys 5 more. Try it:

*Sam starts with 3 stickers. Sam buys 5 more. So Sam has 3 + 5 stickers. And 3 + 5 = 8. So Sam has 8 stickers.* □

<TryIt>
Find something you have some of — grapes, crayons, socks. Add a few more. Now say the whole reason chain out loud, one sentence at a time. Finish with "so." Then say "done!"
</TryIt>
