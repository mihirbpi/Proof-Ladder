Some tellings are about **all** of something. Some are about **some** of it. And some are about **exactly one**. Three shapes, three different jobs.

## Every

> **Every** block in this box is red.

To show this is true, you have to check every single block. Miss one and you have not finished.

To show it is false, you only need to find one block that is not red. One! That is much easier.

## Some

> **Some** block in this box is red. (There is at least one red block.)

Now it flips around. To show this is true, you find one red block and hold it up. Done.

To show it is false, you have to check every single block and show that none of them is red. Now *that* is the hard job.

## Exactly one

> There is **exactly one** red block in this box.

This one is greedy. It wants two things:

1. Find a red block. (There is at least one.)
2. Show there are no others. (There is at most one.)

If you find two red blocks, the telling is false. If you find zero, it is also false. It has to be exactly one.

<Warning title="The hard job swaps sides">
Look at the pattern. For an **every** telling, proving it is hard and knocking it down is easy. For a **some** telling, proving it is easy and knocking it down is hard.

The job is always hard on one side and easy on the other. Which side depends on the word at the front.
</Warning>

<TryIt>
Line up 6 things. Make one *every* claim, one *some* claim, and one *exactly one* claim. Have somebody try to knock each one down. Count how many things they had to pick up each time.
</TryIt>
