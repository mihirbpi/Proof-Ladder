Module 1.1 noted that $x \geq 1$ is not a statement: the $x$ has not been pinned down, so it has no truth value yet. There are two ways to pin it down, and they are called **quantifiers**.

## The *for all* quantifier

The symbol $\forall$ is read "for all" or "for every". A statement built with it claims that something is true of **every** member of a collection you name:
$$
\forall x \in \mathbb{R},\; x^2 \geq 0.
$$

*Symbol by symbol: $\mathbb{R}$ is the real numbers, $\in$ is "is in", $\forall$ is "for every". So the line reads: every number on the line has a square that is zero or bigger.*

And it is true: squaring any number on the line gives something at zero or above.

The set matters. Compare:
$$
\forall x \in \mathbb{Z},\; x \geq 1 \quad \text{(false: } x = 0 \text{)}
\qquad
\forall x \in \mathbb{Z},\; x \geq 1 \text{ or } x \leq 0 \quad \text{(true)}
$$

*Symbol by symbol: $\mathbb{Z}$ is the integers, $\in$ is "is in", $\forall$ is "for every". So the line reads: the first says every integer is at least $1$, which is false because $0$ is not; the second says every integer is at least $1$ or at most $0$, which is true.*

**A claim of this kind is incomplete until you say what collection it runs over.** The same condition can be true of every integer and false of every fraction; the collection is part of the claim, not decoration around it.

## The *there exists* quantifier

The symbol $\exists$ is read "there exists". A statement built with it claims that **at least one** member of the collection has the property:
$$
\exists x \in \mathbb{R},\; 3x - 1 = 0.
$$

*Symbol by symbol: $\mathbb{R}$ is the real numbers, $\in$ is "is in", $\exists$ is "there is some". So the line reads: there is some number on the line for which three times it, minus one, is zero.*

True — take $x = \tfrac13$. Contrast it with
$$
\exists x \in \mathbb{Z},\; 3x - 1 = 0,
$$

*Symbol by symbol: $\mathbb{Z}$ is the integers, $\in$ is "is in", $\exists$ is "there is some". So the line reads: there is some **integer** for which three times it, minus one, is zero.*

which is **false** — no integer works. Same condition, different collection to run it over, different truth value.

<Warning title="There exists never means exactly one">
"There is a number whose square is $4$" is true, and there are two of them. A *there exists* claim says **at least** one, and is completely silent about how many. If you mean exactly one, you have to say so.
</Warning>

## Unique existence

The symbol $\exists!$ is read "there exists exactly one". It packs two separate claims into one mark:

- **at least one** member of the collection has the property, and
- **no two different** members both have it.

Two obligations, and a proof owes you both.

> **Claim.** Exactly one number on the line satisfies $3x - 1 = 0$.

**At least one.** Take $x = \tfrac13$. Then $3 \cdot \tfrac13 - 1 = 0$. ✓

**No two.** Suppose $a$ and $b$ both satisfy it: $3a - 1 = 0$ and $3b - 1 = 0$. Then $3a = 1 = 3b$, so $3a = 3b$, and dividing by $3$ gives $a = b$. The two "different" solutions turned out to be the same number.

The uniqueness pattern — *suppose two things both work, then prove they are equal* — is the standard one, and you will reuse it constantly.

## The evidence each quantifier demands

| Statement | To prove it | To disprove it |
|---|---|---|
| it holds for **every** member | argue about a single member, chosen without assumptions | produce one member where it fails |
| it holds for **some** member | produce one member where it holds | show it fails for every member |

The two rows are mirror images, and the mirror is negation — which is module 1.11. What matters now is the practical consequence: **you cannot prove a universal claim by examples, and you cannot disprove an existential claim by examples.** In each of those cells the work is a general argument, not a search.

## Quantifiers are hidden everywhere

Most theorems are if–then claims made about every member of some collection, with the *for every* left unwritten:

> If $x > 1$ then $x^2 > 1$

means

$$\forall x \in \mathbb{R},\; (x > 1 \Rightarrow x^2 > 1).$$

*Symbol by symbol: $\mathbb{R}$ is the real numbers, $\in$ is "is in", $\forall$ is "for every", $\Rightarrow$ is "forces". So the line reads: for every number on the line: if it is bigger than $1$, then its square is bigger than $1$.*

That invisible *for every* is why the proof in module 1.6 had to reason about a nameless number rather than checking a few values. Making the quantifier visible is often the first useful step on a problem you are stuck on.

## Two quantifiers at once

Statements often carry more than one, and then **order matters**:

$$\forall x \in \mathbb{R},\; \exists y \in \mathbb{R},\; x + y = 0$$

*Symbol by symbol: $\mathbb{R}$ is the real numbers, $\in$ is "is in", $\forall$ is "for every", $\exists$ is "there is some". So the line reads: for every number, there is some number you can add to it to get zero.*

It says: whatever number you hand me, I can find one that cancels it. **True** — take the negative of whatever you were handed, which the second choice is allowed to depend on.

$$\exists y \in \mathbb{R},\; \forall x \in \mathbb{R},\; x + y = 0$$

*Symbol by symbol: $\mathbb{R}$ is the real numbers, $\in$ is "is in", $\forall$ is "for every", $\exists$ is "there is some". So the line reads: there is one single number that gives zero when added to every number.*

It says: there is one single number that cancels **everything** at once. **False** — no fixed number cancels both $1$ and $2$.

## Which one comes first decides everything

Same symbols, same condition, opposite verdicts — and the only difference is the order.

The rule of thumb: **a later choice may depend on an earlier one; an earlier choice may not depend on a later one.** Reading the line left to right is reading the order the choices get made in.

This ordering subtlety is not a technicality; it is one of the hardest things in the mathematics ahead of you. Two important definitions in a later course differ by exactly one swap of *for every* and *there is* — and a reader who has not taken this module seriously cannot see the difference at all.
