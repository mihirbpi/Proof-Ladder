You've spent the year writing two-column geometry proofs, where every line is either a *given*, a *statement*, or a *reason*. This module fixes what "statement" means precisely enough that the rest of Chapter 1 can build on it.

## Definition

A **statement** is a declarative sentence with exactly one **truth value**: either T (true) or F (false). Sentences without a well-defined truth value — questions, commands, or expressions — are not statements.

Examples that *are* statements:

- $7$ is prime. (T)
- $8$ is prime. (F)
- $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational. (T, and it's the whole point of §4.7)
- Every even integer $\geq 4$ is the sum of two primes. (unknown truth value — but still a statement)
- The polynomial $x^2 + 1$ has no real root. (T)

Examples that are *not* statements:

- *Is $7$ prime?* (question)
- *Prove $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.* (command)
- $x^2 + 1$ (an expression — no verb, nothing to be true about)
- "the smallest odd prime" (a noun phrase; refers to $3$, but doesn't assert anything)

## Statements about variables

Consider $x^2 \geq 0$. Is this a statement? On its own, no — the sentence contains a free variable $x$ whose truth-value depends on the value chosen. We call such a sentence an **open statement**. It becomes a genuine statement in two natural ways:

- **Substitute:** $3^2 \geq 0$ is a statement (T).
- **Quantify:** $\forall x \in \mathbb{R},\; x^2 \geq 0$ is a statement (T). Module 1.10 introduces the $\forall$ notation.

## Conjectures

Many important statements have unknown truth value. Two you should meet by name:

- **Goldbach's Conjecture.** Every even integer $\geq 4$ is the sum of two primes. Verified computationally up to $4 \times 10^{18}$; unproved.
- **Twin Primes Conjecture.** There are infinitely many primes $p$ with $p + 2$ also prime. Recent work (Zhang, 2013; Maynard, 2013) showed there are infinitely many prime pairs at bounded distance, but not that the gap can be $2$.

Both are statements. Nobody has settled their truth value; that doesn't stop them from being statements. A **conjecture** is a statement believed true but not yet proved.

Sometimes conjectures fall:

- **Fermat's Last Theorem** (Fermat, 1637): a conjecture for 358 years, proved by Wiles in 1995.
- **Pólya's conjecture** (1919): believed for decades, disproved by counterexample in 1958.

<Warning title="Not-yet-known is not the same as not-true">
A conjecture's truth value is a fact about the world, whether or not we've settled it. "It hasn't been proved" is a statement about *us*, not about the mathematics.
</Warning>

<Aside>
Some statements can't be settled from a given axiom system at all — this is Gödel's insight. Chapter 7 will meet the Peano axioms; l8 of this module points at the deeper story.
</Aside>
