A **telling** is a sentence that says something is so. You can go and check it.

Here are four tellings:

- Noodle the cat has four legs.
- It is raining outside.
- Two plus three equals five.
- Noodle the cat is bright blue.

Three of those are true and one is not. (Sorry, Noodle.) But all four are tellings, because you could find out which is which.

These are **not** tellings:

- Please pass the water.
- Is your name Sam?
- Yikes!

The first one asks you to do something. The second one is a question. The third one is a noise a person makes when a dog steals their sandwich. None of them can be true or false, so none of them are tellings.

## Easy checks and hard checks

If somebody says *the sky is blue*, you just look up. Easy.

If somebody says *there is a lost penny under Sam's bed*, you have to get down on the floor with a flashlight. Harder. But it is still a telling! Hard to check does not mean not a telling.

And here is the wild part. There are tellings that nobody on the whole planet has figured out yet. Grown-ups have been checking some of them for two hundred years. They are still tellings. Nobody has finished checking.
