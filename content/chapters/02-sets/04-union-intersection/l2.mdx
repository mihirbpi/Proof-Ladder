When you draw two hoops that cross, the picture has a name: a **Venn diagram**. It is one of the most useful pictures in all of math, and you can draw it in about four seconds.

Let box $S$ be the animals with four legs. Let box $T$ be the animals with fur.

## The overlap: in both

<Figure alt="Two overlapping circles labeled S and T with only the middle overlap region shaded." caption="The overlap: in S and in T.">
  <VennTwo labelS="S" labelT="T" shade={["both"]} />
</Figure>

A cat has four legs **and** fur, so the cat sits in the **overlap**.

A snake has fur? No. A bird has four legs? No. Neither one gets into the middle.

The overlap is picky. To get in, you have to pass both tests.

## All together: in at least one

<Figure alt="Two overlapping circles labeled S and T with both circles entirely shaded." caption="All together: in S or in T, or both.">
  <VennTwo labelS="S" labelT="T" shade={["S-only", "T-only", "both"]} />
</Figure>

Now shade both hoops completely. That is everything in $S$ **or** in $T$ — **all together**.

A cat gets in (both!). A bird gets in (four legs? no — but wait, a bird has two legs. Let's say a horse instead). A horse gets in. A hairless cat gets in, because it still has four legs.

Remember module 1.3: *or* means at least one, and both is fine. So the overlap is part of "all together" too.

## Hoops that never overlap

<Figure alt="Two circles side by side that do not touch, labeled S and T." caption="These two never overlap: nothing is in both.">
  <VennTwo labelS="S" labelT="T" disjoint={true} />
</Figure>

Now let $S$ be the even numbers and $T$ be the odd numbers. Is anything in both?

No! No number is both even and odd. These two hoops **never overlap**. Draw them apart, not crossing.
