Sam has 2 shirts and 3 hats. To make an outfit, Sam picks one shirt **and** one hat. That is a **pair**.

## Draw the grid

Put the shirts down the side. Put the hats along the top.

Every box is one outfit. Count them: 6 boxes, 6 outfits.

## Counting without drawing

You do not have to draw the whole grid. Look at the shape of it.

There are 2 rows with 3 boxes in each. So count $3$, then $3$ more — that is $6$ boxes.

So you can count a grid by adding one row over and over. A grid with $4$ rows of $5$ has $5$ and $5$ and $5$ and $5$ — that is $20$ boxes, and you never had to draw a single one.

## A thing made of two things

Here is something new. Up until now, everything in a box was one thing — one block, one number, one animal.

Now the things in the box are **pairs**. Each one is made of two parts stuck together, and it stays stuck. *Red shirt with party hat* is one outfit, not two.

And the order matters. The shirt part comes first, the hat part comes second. If you swapped them you would be wearing a hat as a shirt, and that is a different day entirely.

<Aside>
You have met pairs before without noticing. On a treasure map, "3 across and 2 up" is a pair. Swap it to "2 across and 3 up" and you dig in the wrong place. First slot, second slot — they do different jobs.
</Aside>
