Back in module 1.4 you found a rule with a hat and a coat: crossing out an AND gives you an OR. Now watch the same rule show up in a picture.

Two hoops: $S$ and $T$.

## Outside both hoops

<Figure alt="Two overlapping circles S and T inside a dashed box, with only the region outside both circles shaded." caption="Outside $S$ and outside $T$ — at the same time.">
  <VennTwo labelS="S" labelT="T" shade={["outside"]} universe="everything" />
</Figure>

Shade everything that is not in $S$ and not in $T$. You get the border around the edge.

Now here is the same shape said another way: it is everything that is **not in ($S$ or $T$)**. If a thing is not in either hoop, it is out there in the border.

So:

> Outside ($S$ **or** $T$) = outside $S$ **and** outside $T$.

The OR turned into an AND!

## Outside the overlap

Now try the other one. Shade everything that is **not in the overlap**.

That is a lot of shading. Everything except the little middle lens.

Who is out there? Things in $S$ but not $T$. Things in $T$ but not $S$. And things in neither. What do all of those have in common?

Each one is missing at least one hoop. It is not in $S$, **or** it is not in $T$.

> Outside ($S$ **and** $T$) = outside $S$ **or** outside $T$.

The AND turned into an OR!

<Warning title="The swap, again">
This is exactly the hat-and-coat rule from module 1.4. When you go to the outside, the little joining word always flips. AND becomes OR. OR becomes AND.

It is the same rule. Once you learn it with hats, you know it with hoops.
</Warning>

<TryIt>
Draw two circles that cross. Shade everything outside both. Now, on a new drawing, shade everything outside just the middle lens. Compare the two pictures — they are not the same!
</TryIt>
