There are two ways to say what is in a box.

## The list-box

You name everything in it:

> $A$ is the box with $2$, $4$, $6$, $8$, and $10$ in it.

Clear. No arguing. But you have to write out every single thing.

## The rule-box

You give a rule instead:

> $A$ is the box of all even numbers from $1$ to $10$.

Shorter! And it says exactly the same thing. Check it: $2$ is even and between $1$ and $10$ — in. $7$ is not even — out. $12$ is even but too big — out.

Same box, two ways of saying it.

## When you really need a rule

Now try this one.

> $B$ is the box of **all** even numbers.

Could you make that a list-box? You would write $2$, $4$, $6$, $8$, $10$, $12$… and you would never stop. Not today, not ever.

But the rule takes four words. *All the even numbers.* Done.

That is why rule-boxes matter. Some boxes are far too big to list, and a rule is the only way to get hold of them.

<Aside>
A good rule has to be a telling (module 1.1) about each thing. *Is $7$ even?* has an answer, so "all even numbers" is a fine rule. *Is $7$ a fun number?* has no answer, so "all the fun numbers" is not a box at all.
</Aside>
