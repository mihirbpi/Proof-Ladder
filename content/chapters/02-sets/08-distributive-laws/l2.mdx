Three hoops: $S$, $T$, and $R$. Here is a job you can do two different ways, and both ways give the same answer.

## The job

Find everything that is in $S$ **and** also in ($T$ or $R$).

## Way one: all at once

First join $T$ and $R$ into one big blob. Then find where $S$ overlaps that blob.

## Way two: share it out

Do $S$ with $T$. Then do $S$ with $R$. Then join your two answers.

## They match

Try it with a toy. Say a red car is in $S$ and in $R$, but not in $T$.

- Way one: is the car in $S$? Yes. Is it in ($T$ or $R$)? Yes, it's in $R$. So it counts.
- Way two: is it in ($S$ and $T$)? No. Is it in ($S$ and $R$)? Yes! So it counts.

Both ways said yes. And that happens for every single toy, no matter where it sits.

So you can **share it out** whenever you like. Pick whichever way is easier to do.

<Aside>
You have seen sharing out before. $3 \times (2 + 4)$ is the same as $(3 \times 2) + (3 \times 4)$ — both give $18$. Hoops do the same thing. But hoops have a bonus that numbers do not, and module 2.8 at higher levels will show you what it is.
</Aside>

<TryIt>
Draw three circles that all cross. Shade *$S$ and ($T$ or $R$)*. Then, on a fresh drawing, shade *($S$ and $T$)* and *($S$ and $R$)*. Hold the two drawings up to a window and check they match.
</TryIt>
