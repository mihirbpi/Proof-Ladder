The **outside** of a box is everything else. But "everything else" is a slippery phrase, so you have to say what you are counting first.

Let box $A$ be the red blocks.

- If we are counting **just the blocks**, the outside of $A$ is the blue blocks, green blocks, and yellow blocks.
- If we are counting **everything in the room**, the outside of $A$ also has the lamp, the cat, and your shoes.

Same box $A$. Two different outsides. You must always say which one you mean.

<Warning title="Say the whole first">
"Everything not in $A$" is not a finished sentence. Everything not in $A$ *out of what?* Answer that, then take the outside.
</Warning>

## A brand new trick: suppose not

Here is one of the best moves in all of mathematics, and it works like a dare.

> **Claim.** All the red things are in the box. So nothing outside the box is red.

How would you show that? Try it backwards. **Suppose not.**

<Proof level="l2">
Suppose there IS a red thing outside the box. Go on, imagine it. A red sock, sitting on the floor.

But we said all the red things are in the box.

So the red sock has to be in the box.

But the sock is outside the box! It cannot be inside and outside at the same time.

That is impossible. So there was never a red sock out there at all.
</Proof>

Do you see the shape? You pretend the opposite is true, follow it until it says something silly, and then you know the opposite was wrong all along.

That move is called **suppose not**, and you will use it for the rest of your life.

<TryIt>
Hide all the red things in a box. Now ask a grown-up to find a red thing outside the box. Watch them fail. Then say why they had to fail.
</TryIt>
