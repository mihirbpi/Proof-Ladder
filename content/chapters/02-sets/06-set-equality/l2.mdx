Two boxes are **the same box** when they hold exactly the same things. Not similar. Not close. Exactly.

And to show that, you have to **check both boxes** — the same move you learned in module 1.9.

| Check | What you do |
|---|---|
| ☐ 1 | Everything in box $A$ is also in box $B$. |
| ☐ 2 | Everything in box $B$ is also in box $A$. |

Both checks, or nothing.

## Why one check is not enough

Say $A$ is the box with a cat and a dog. Say $B$ is the box with a cat, a dog, and a duck.

Check 1: is everything in $A$ also in $B$? Cat — yes. Dog — yes. **Passed!**

If you stopped now, you would say the boxes are the same. But they are not! $B$ has a duck.

Check 2 catches it: the duck is in $B$ and not in $A$. **Failed.**

That is why you always do both.

## A number one

Let $A$ be the box of numbers you get by doubling: $2, 4, 6, 8, 10, \ldots$

Let $B$ be the box of even numbers.

Are they the same box?

<Proof level="l2">
**Check 1.** Take any one thing from $A$. It is some number doubled. Doubling makes two equal piles, so it is even. So it is in $B$. ✓

**Check 2.** Take any one thing from $B$. It is even, so it splits into two equal piles. Take one pile — that is the number we doubled. So it is in $A$. ✓

Both checks passed. So $A$ and $B$ are the same box.
</Proof>

<Warning title="Looking the same is not the same">
Two boxes can be described in totally different words and still be the same box. *Doubled numbers* and *even numbers* sound different. They are not. The only thing that matters is what is inside.
</Warning>

<TryIt>
Describe a group of things two different ways — say, *the things I can eat* and *the things in the fruit bowl*. Then do check 1 and check 2 and see if the boxes really match.
</TryIt>
