## One cubby at a time

**Trace back** from cubby 1: you get Sam.

Trace back from cubby 2: you get Pip and Ada.

## Two cubbies at once

Now put cubbies 1 and 2 together into one little group. Trace back from that whole group.

Who lands in cubby 1 or cubby 2? Sam, Pip, and Ada.

And look — that is exactly Sam, joined with Pip and Ada.

> Trace back from a group = trace back from each one, then join.

## Try the overlap

Now try it the other way. Take the *overlap* of two groups instead of joining them.

Cubbies $\{1, 2\}$ and cubbies $\{2, 3\}$ overlap at cubby 2.

- Trace back from $\{1,2\}$: Sam, Pip, Ada.
- Trace back from $\{2,3\}$: Pip, Ada, Bo.
- What is in *both* of those answers? Pip and Ada.
- And tracing back from the overlap $\{2\}$ gives: Pip and Ada. Same!

It works for overlaps too. And it works for outsides too.

Tracing back gets along with hoops perfectly, every single time.

<Aside>
Going *forwards* is not so tidy. If you circle two groups of children and look at where they land, the overlap can go wrong — two different children can land in the same cubby. You will see that carefully in a few years. For now: backwards is the well-behaved direction.
</Aside>
