Some machines are tidier than others. Here is the tidiness this module is about.

## A sharing machine

<Figure alt="Three children on the left; Sam and Pip both have arrows landing on cubby 2, Ada goes to cubby 1, and cubby 3 is unused." caption="Sam and Pip both land on cubby 2. That is sharing.">
  <ArrowDiagram labelLeft="children" labelRight="cubbies"
    left={["Sam", "Pip", "Ada"]} right={["1", "2", "3"]}
    edges={[[0,1],[1,1],[2,0]]} />

Two arrows land on cubby 2. That is a **sharing** machine.

## A no-sharing machine

<Figure alt="Three children on the left, each with an arrow landing on a different cubby; no two arrows land together." caption="Every arrow lands somewhere different. No sharing!">
  <ArrowDiagram labelLeft="children" labelRight="cubbies"
    left={["Sam", "Pip", "Ada"]} right={["1", "2", "3"]}
    edges={[[0,1],[1,0],[2,2]]} />
</Figure>

Now no two arrows land in the same place. This is a **no-sharing machine**.

## How to spot one

Look at the *right* side. Count the arrows landing on each thing.

- 0 arrows? Fine.
- 1 arrow? Fine.
- 2 or more arrows? **Sharing.** Not a no-sharing machine.

So: every landing spot must have at most one arrow.

## Why it matters

In a no-sharing machine, you can always work backwards.

Somebody hands you a coat from cubby 2. Whose is it? In the tidy machine, there is exactly one answer. In the sharing machine, you are stuck.

<Warning title="Check the right side, not the left">
In module 3.1, you checked the *left* side to see if something was a machine at all. Now you check the *right* side to see if it is tidy. Different question, different side.
</Warning>

## A number one

Try the doubling machine: put in a number, get double.

$1 \to 2$. $2 \to 4$. $3 \to 6$.

Does anything share? Could two different numbers double to the same thing? No! If your answers are the same, you must have started with the same number.

So doubling is a no-sharing machine.

Now try the last-digit machine: put in a number, get its last digit.

Put in $27$, and you get $7$. Put in $17$, and you also get $7$.

Uh oh. Two different starts, one answer. **Sharing!**
