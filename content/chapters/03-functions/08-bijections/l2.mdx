A machine with **both** is a **perfect pairing**.

## Counting without counting

Here is why this matters so much.

Suppose you have a huge pile of forks and a huge pile of knives. Which pile is bigger?

You *could* count them both. Slow, and you might lose your place.

Or you could pair them up. Take a fork, take a knife, put them together. Again. Again.

- Ran out of forks first? More knives.
- Ran out of knives first? More forks.
- Ran out at the same time? **Same number!**

You never counted. You just paired.

## Why this is a big deal

Little kids do this before they can count at all, and grown-up mathematicians do it too — because sometimes counting is not just slow, it is impossible.

How many whole numbers are there? You cannot count them. They never stop.

But you can still *pair them up* with things. And when mathematicians want to compare two never-ending piles, pairing is the only tool that works.

<Aside>
Here is something strange to think about. Pair up every whole number with its double: 1 with 2, 2 with 4, 3 with 6, and so on forever. Nothing is shared and nothing is left out on the right, because every even number is somebody's double. So there are just as many even numbers as there are whole numbers — even though the even numbers seem like only half of them! Never-ending piles do not behave like ordinary piles.
</Aside>
