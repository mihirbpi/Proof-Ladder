A function runs one way: input in, output out. But you can ask questions in both directions, and the two directions get different names.

## The image: what actually comes out

The **image** of a function $f : S \to T$ is the set of answers it actually produces:
$$
\text{Im}(f) = \{\, f(s) \mid s \in S \,\}.
$$

In the picture, $\text{Im}(f) = \{1, 2, 4\}$. The element $3$ is in the codomain but not in the image, because nothing gets sent there.

So the image is always a subset of the codomain — sometimes the whole thing, often not:
$$
\text{Im}(f) \subseteq T.
$$

## Codomain versus image

This distinction trips everybody up once, so here it is plainly.

- The **codomain** is what you *declared* when you set the function up. It is a promise about where answers will land.
- The **image** is what actually *happens*. It is a fact about the function, not a choice.

You choose the codomain. You do not choose the image; you compute it.

## Worked example: the alphabet function

Let $f$ send each letter to its position, with codomain the counting numbers:
$$
f(\text{a}) = 1, \quad \ldots, \quad f(\text{z}) = 26.
$$

The codomain is all the counting numbers. But the image is only
$$
\text{Im}(f) = \{1, 2, 3, \ldots, 26\}.
$$
Nothing maps to $27$, or $100$, or a million. Same function, and the two sets are wildly different sizes.

## The pre-image: tracing back

Pick a set $U$ inside the codomain. The **pre-image** of $U$ is everything in the domain that lands inside $U$:
$$
f^{-1}(U) = \{\, s \in S \mid f(s) \in U \,\}.
$$

From the picture: $f^{-1}(\{2\}) = \{a, b\}$, because both $a$ and $b$ point at $2$. And $f^{-1}(\{1\}) = \{c\}$.

Now the interesting one:
$$
f^{-1}(\{3\}) = \emptyset.
$$
Nothing lands on $3$, so tracing back from it gives you the empty set. **This is a correct answer, not an error.** Pre-images are allowed to be empty, and they often are.

For the alphabet function:
$$
f^{-1}(\{5\}) = \{\text{e}\},
\qquad
f^{-1}(\{27\}) = \emptyset,
\qquad
f^{-1}(\{1,2,3\}) = \{\text{a}, \text{b}, \text{c}\}.
$$

## Image and pre-image are linked

Here is the connection, and it is worth remembering:
$$
t \in \text{Im}(f) \quad\text{exactly when}\quad f^{-1}(\{t\}) \neq \emptyset.
$$

An element is in the image precisely when something traces back to it. Both statements say "$t$ gets hit"; one says it going forwards and one going backwards.

<Warning title="f⁻¹ does not mean the function runs backwards">
The notation $f^{-1}$ looks like it should undo $f$. It does not — not yet. Right now $f^{-1}(U)$ is defined for **every** function, tidy or messy, and it always takes a *set* and gives back a *set*.

In our picture, $f^{-1}(\{2\}) = \{a, b\}$ — two things. No single-valued undo-machine could do that. Module 3.9 sorts out exactly when a real undo-machine exists.
</Warning>

<Aside>
Pre-images turn out to be far better behaved than images, which is why mathematicians reach for them constantly. Module 3.4 shows that pre-images get along perfectly with unions, intersections, and complements — and images do not.
</Aside>
