There are two questions you can ask about any machine, and they go in opposite directions.

## Going forwards: circle the arrowheads

Look at the right side. Which cubbies have an arrow landing on them?

Cubby 1 — yes. Cubby 2 — yes, twice! Cubby 4 — yes. Cubby 3 — no arrows at all.

**Circle the arrowheads** and you get $\{1, 2, 4\}$. Those are the answers the machine can actually give.

Cubby 3 is not in the circle. The machine simply never sends anybody there.

## Going backwards: trace back

Now pick a cubby and follow the arrows the wrong way.

- Trace back from cubby 2: you land on Sam and Pip. Two children!
- Trace back from cubby 1: you land on Ada. One child.
- Trace back from cubby 3: you land on... nobody. Zero children.

All three of those are real answers. "Nobody" is an answer, not a mistake.

## The two questions are different

*Which cubbies got used?* is about the whole right side at once.

*Who is in cubby 3?* is about one cubby.

Both are useful, and later on they turn into two of the most important ideas in the whole chapter.

<Aside>
Notice that tracing back can give you 0 things, 1 thing, or lots of things. The machine going forwards is very tidy — always exactly one answer. Going backwards is messy. That difference is what modules 3.5 and 3.6 are all about.
</Aside>
