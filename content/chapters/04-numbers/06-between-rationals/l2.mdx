Give me two numbers and I will find one **halfway** between them.

$4$ and $6$? Halfway is $5$.

$10$ and $20$? Halfway is $15$.

## How to find halfway

Add the two numbers, then share the answer between two.

$4 + 6 = 10$, and $10$ shared between two is $5$. ✓

$3 + 8 = 11$, and $11$ shared between two is $5\tfrac12$. ✓

That is the whole recipe. Add, then halve.

## Now do it again

$3$ and $4$. Halfway is $3\tfrac12$.

$3$ and $3\tfrac12$. Halfway is $3\tfrac14$.

$3$ and $3\tfrac14$. Halfway is $3\tfrac18$.

$3$ and $3\tfrac18$. Halfway is $3\tfrac{1}{16}$.

You can keep going. Forever. Every single time there is a new number sitting in the gap.

## Why you never run out

Here is the reason, and it is short.

Whatever two different numbers you pick, the halfway point is a real number, and it is bigger than the small one and smaller than the big one. So it is genuinely a *new* number in the gap.

Then take the small one and your new one, and do it again. There is no way for this to stop.

<Aside>
This is very different from counting numbers. Between $4$ and $5$ there is no *whole* number at all — the gap is empty. But once fractions are allowed, no gap is ever empty. Fractions fill the ruler in a way whole numbers never could.
</Aside>
