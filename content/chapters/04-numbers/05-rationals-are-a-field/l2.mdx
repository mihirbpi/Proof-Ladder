In module 4.1 you found a hole. Adding whole numbers keeps you inside the whole numbers, and so does making equal groups of them. Sharing throws you out.

Share $7$ counters between $2$ people and one is left over. So what is that leftover half?

It is a **fraction**. And fractions are exactly the numbers people invented to fill that hole.

## Fractions come from sharing

$7$ cookies, $2$ friends. Give each friend $3$, and snap the last one in half.

Each friend gets $3$ and a half. That is the answer to sharing $7$ between $2$, and now it *is* a number.

## Fractions do not have the hole

Here is the wonderful part. Try to escape the fractions.

**Add two fractions.** A half plus a quarter is three quarters. Still a fraction!

**Multiply two fractions.** A half times a half is a quarter. Still a fraction!

**Share one fraction into another.** How many quarters fit inside a half? Two of them. And $2$ is a fraction too — two whole ones.

You cannot get out. Whatever you do, you land back among the fractions.

(One exception, and it is the only one: you can never divide by zero. Sharing $5$ cookies among $0$ people is not a question with an answer.)

## The missing piece the whole numbers lacked

Take three quarters of a pizza. Now flip that fraction upside down — four thirds.

Put those two together the equal-groups way and you land exactly on one whole. Every time!

Whole numbers cannot do that. Take $3$ whole cookies — how many copies of them make exactly one cookie? You would need less than one copy, and there is no whole number smaller than one except zero.

But a **third** works: three thirds make exactly one whole. And a third is a fraction.

That flipping trick is exactly what fractions have and whole numbers do not.

<Aside>
Grown-up mathematicians have a name for a number system where you can add, take opposites, make equal groups, *and* flip everything except zero. They call it a **field**. The whole numbers are not a field. The fractions are.
</Aside>
