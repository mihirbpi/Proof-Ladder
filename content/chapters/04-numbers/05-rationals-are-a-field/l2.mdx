In module 4.1 you found a hole. Adding and multiplying whole numbers keeps you inside the whole numbers. Dividing throws you out.

$7 \div 2$ is not a whole number. So what is it?

It is a **fraction**. And fractions are exactly the numbers people invented to fill that hole.

## Fractions come from sharing

$7$ cookies, $2$ friends. Give each friend $3$, and snap the last one in half.

Each friend gets $3$ and a half. That is the answer to $7 \div 2$, and now it *is* a number.

## Fractions do not have the hole

Here is the wonderful part. Try to escape the fractions.

**Add two fractions.** A half plus a quarter is three quarters. Still a fraction!

**Multiply two fractions.** A half times a half is a quarter. Still a fraction!

**Divide two fractions.** A half divided by a quarter is $2$ — and $2$ is a fraction too, because $2$ is $\tfrac21$.

You cannot get out. Whatever you do, you land back among the fractions.

(One exception, and it is the only one: you can never divide by zero. Sharing $5$ cookies among $0$ people is not a question with an answer.)

## The missing piece the whole numbers lacked

Take any fraction — say $\tfrac34$. Now flip it upside down: $\tfrac43$.

Multiply them together and you get $1$. Every time!

Whole numbers cannot do that. What whole number times $3$ gives $1$? None. But $\tfrac13$ times $3$ gives $1$, and $\tfrac13$ is a fraction.

That flipping trick is exactly what fractions have and whole numbers do not.

<Aside>
Grown-up mathematicians have a name for a number system where you can add, take opposites, multiply, *and* flip everything except zero. They call it a **field**. The whole numbers are not a field. The fractions are.
</Aside>
