Here is a question you have probably never been asked. When you do arithmetic, **can you get out?**

## Adding keeps you in

Start with whole numbers. Add two of them.

$3 + 4 = 7$. Whole.
$12 + 88 = 100$. Whole.
$0 + 6 = 6$. Whole.

Try as hard as you like. You will never add two whole numbers and get half of something. Adding cannot get you out.

We say the whole numbers are **closed** under adding. Closed means: you always land back where you started.

## Multiplying keeps you in too

Try counting by equal groups. $4$ and $4$ and $4$ is $12$. Whole!

Take $10$ groups of nothing. You still have nothing, which is $0$. Whole!

Also closed. No escape.

## Dividing lets you out!

Share $6$ between $2$ people. They get $3$ each. Whole — fine so far.

Now share $7$ between $2$ people. They get $3$ each, and there is **one left over**. To be fair you must cut it.

There it is. You started with two whole numbers and landed on something that is *not* a whole number. You got **out**.

So the whole numbers are **not** closed under dividing.

<Warning title="Not closed does not mean never">
Sometimes sharing works out fine. $6$ shared between $2$ is a perfectly good whole number.

But **closed** means it works *every* time, with no exceptions. And one escape is enough to break it — that is one bad apple from module 1.11.
</Warning>

## Why anyone cares

Because that hole is where new numbers come from.

The whole numbers could not handle sharing $7$ between $2$. So people invented pieces to fill the gap. Later on, pieces could not handle some other question, so people invented more numbers again.

Every time you meet a new kind of number, it is because somebody found a hole like this one.
