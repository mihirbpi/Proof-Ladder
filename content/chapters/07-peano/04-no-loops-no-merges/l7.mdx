$$
\textbf{A7}\quad \forall x,\ S(x) \neq 0; \qquad\qquad
\textbf{A8}\quad \forall x,y,\ S(x)=S(y)\Rightarrow x=y.
$$

Structurally: $0 \notin \operatorname{Im}(S)$ (§3.3) and $S$ is injective (§3.5). Together $S$ is an injection $\mathbb{N}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}$.

By soundness (§7.1), a structure satisfying $\Gamma$ and refuting $\varphi$ witnesses $\Gamma\not\vdash\varphi$. So neither A7 nor A8 is derivable from the remaining axioms, and both earn their place.

More generally, the models of A5–A6 alone are exactly the *pointed dynamical systems* $(M, 0, S)$ — any set with a distinguished point and any endofunction whatsoever. A7 and A8 cut that class down to those in which the orbit of $0$ is an infinite simple path.

## The numerals are distinct

> **Proposition.** Under A5–A8, $S^m(0) \neq S^n(0)$ for $m \neq n$.

<Proof>
Suppose $S^m(0)=S^n(0)$ with $m<n$. Applying A8 $m$ times cancels, giving $0 = S^{\,n-m}(0)$ with $n-m\geq 1$. Then $0 = S\big(S^{\,n-m-1}(0)\big)$ is a successor, contradicting A7.
</Proof>

A8 supplied the cancellation, A7 the contradiction. The two models above show that neither contribution can be dispensed with.

Consequently $n \mapsto S^n(0)$ is an injection $\mathbb{N}_{\text{meta}} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{N}$, and $\mathbb{N}$ is infinite.

Moreover $S : \mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ is injective but not surjective (its image omits $0$), so $\mathbb{N}$ is in bijection with the proper subset $\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}$ — i.e. **Dedekind infinite** (§3.8). Dedekind took this as the *definition* of infinity in 1888, and A7–A8 are exactly the axioms making $\mathbb{N}$ satisfy it.

<Warning title="A5–A8 do not pin down ℕ">
Let $\mathbb{N}' = \{0,1,2,\ldots\} \sqcup \{a,b\}$ with the standard successor on the numerals and $S(a)=b$, $S(b)=a$.

A5 ✓, A6 ✓, A7 ✓ (neither $a$ nor $b$ maps to $0$), A8 ✓ ($S$ is injective throughout).

So a model may contain a full copy of $\mathbb{N}$ *plus* extra components disconnected from $0$. A5–A8 constrain the component of $0$ and say nothing about the rest.

§7.5's Axiom 9 is precisely the minimality condition excluding these. The instructive point is that no *finitely many* first-order axioms of this kind can do the job — see below.
</Warning>

<Aside>
A7 and A8 hold in models with rogue elements, and A9 will exclude them — but only in its **second-order** form, quantifying over all subsets. The first-order induction schema (one axiom per formula) cannot: by compactness, adding a constant $c$ and the axioms $c \neq \overline n$ for every numeral $\overline n$ yields a consistent theory, hence a model containing an element exceeding every numeral.

These are the **nonstandard models** of arithmetic. They satisfy every first-order theorem of $\mathbb{N}$ and are not isomorphic to it — so the notion "natural number" is not first-order definable. Skolem constructed the first in 1934.
</Aside>

<WhereThisGoes level="l7">
A7 and A8 say the orbit of $0$ under $S$ is an embedded copy of a ray, and that shape recurs whenever a structure is generated freely by one operation: free monoids on one generator, the integers as a free group on one generator, and polynomial rings $R[x]$ as free $R$-algebras on one generator are all the same phenomenon — no relations beyond those forced. Categorically, freeness is a universal property, and $\mathbb{N}$ is the free monoid on one generator, the initial algebra for $X \mapsto 1+X$, and the natural numbers object of **Set**, all of which are the same statement.

The failure of first-order axioms to pin down $\mathbb{N}$ is the other thread. Nonstandard models are not pathologies to be avoided; Robinson's nonstandard analysis uses the analogous models of $\mathbb{R}$ to make infinitesimals rigorous, giving a treatment of calculus in which $dx$ is a genuine number and the classical proofs become the intuitive ones Leibniz wrote.
</WhereThisGoes>
