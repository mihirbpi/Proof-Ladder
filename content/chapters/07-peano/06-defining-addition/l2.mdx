You have zero, and you have a next-button. That is all you have. So how do you build **adding** out of them?

With **two rules for adding**.

**Rule A.** Adding zero does nothing. You start somewhere and press the button zero times, so you stay put.

**Rule B.** To add the next-one-after-something, add the something first, and then press once more.

That is it. Every sum in the world comes from those two rules.

## Doing $1 + 1$ the slow way

You know the answer is $2$. But watch it get *built*.

Remember what the names mean: $1$ is the next one after $0$. And $2$ is the next one after $1$.

<Proof level="l2">
We want $1 + 1$.

The second $1$ is *the next one after 0*. So Rule B applies:

> add $0$ first, then press once more.

$1 + 0$ is just $1$, by Rule A.

Now press once more. The next one after $1$ is... $2$!

So $1 + 1 = 2$. □
</Proof>

## You just proved that

Stop and notice what happened.

$1+1 = 2$ was not something we looked up or agreed on. It came out of the rules. It is a little thing we **showed**, with reasons.

That is the whole point of Chapter 7. Things you have known since you were tiny turn out to have reasons underneath them.

## Try $2 + 2$

<Proof level="l2">
The second $2$ is the next one after $1$. So by Rule B: do $2 + 1$ first, then press once more.

And $2+1$? The $1$ is the next one after $0$. So by Rule B again: do $2 + 0$ first, then press.

$2 + 0 = 2$, by Rule A.

Press once: that gives $3$. So $2 + 1 = 3$.

Press once more: that gives $4$. So $2 + 2 = 4$. □
</Proof>

Every sum unwinds down to Rule A, and then you press your way back up.

<Aside>
Here is something that will surprise you.

Is $2 + 3$ the same as $3 + 2$? You have always known it is. But look at the two rules — neither one says anything about swapping!

So swapping is **not** free. It has to be proved, and the proof uses the dominoes from Chapter 6. Something you thought was obvious turns out to need work.
</Aside>
