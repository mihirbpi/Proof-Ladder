Module 7.6 built adding out of the next-button. Now we build **times** out of adding.

Again, **two rules for times**.

**Rule M1.** Anything times zero is zero. Zero groups means nothing at all.

**Rule M2.** To do *something times the next-one-after-$b$*, do *something times $b$* first, and then add one more copy.

## Doing three groups of two

<Proof level="l2">
Remember: $2$ is the next one after $1$, and $1$ is the next one after $0$.

We want *three, two times over*. By Rule M2, do *three, one time over* first, then add one more $3$.

*Three, one time over*. By Rule M2 again, do *three, zero times over* first, then add one more $3$.

*Three, zero times over* is $0$, by Rule M1.

Add a $3$: that gives $3$. So *three, one time over* is $3$.

Add another $3$: that gives $6$. So *three, two times over* is $6$. □
</Proof>

Six! Which is what your counters said.

## Times one

Here is a small thing worth proving.

<Proof level="l2">
Take any number and multiply it by $1$.

$1$ is the next one after $0$. So by Rule M2: do (the number) times $0$ first, then add one more copy.

Times zero gives $0$, by Rule M1.

Then add one copy of the number. Starting from zero and adding the number gives... the number!

So anything times one is itself. □
</Proof>

You have always known that. Now you have a reason.

## The tower

Look at what has been built, and in what order.

**Bottom:** zero, and a next-button. That is all we started with.

**Next floor up:** adding. Built out of pressing next, over and over.

**Top floor:** times. Built out of adding, over and over.

Each floor stands on the one below. And the whole tower rests on two little things: a starting point and a button.

<Aside>
Every rule you have ever used in arithmetic sits somewhere in that tower.

Three groups of four having the same total as four groups of three? A theorem. Three rows of ($2$ red and $5$ blue) matching three rows of red plus three rows of blue? A theorem.

Each one has a proof, and each proof uses the dominoes from Chapter 6.

Nothing was ever just true because somebody said so.
</Aside>
