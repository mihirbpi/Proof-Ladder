Two rules build all the counting numbers. Just two.

**Rule 1.** Zero is a counting number.

**Rule 2.** Every counting number has a **next** one, and that next one is a counting number too.

That is it. From those two rules, everything.

## Building the numbers

Start with $0$. Rule 1 says it is there.

What comes next after $0$? Rule 2 says there is something. Let's give it a **name**. We will call it **one**.

What comes next after one? Rule 2 says there is something. Call it **two**.

Next after two? Call it **three**.

And on and on, forever.

## The names are our choice

Here is the part worth thinking about.

The names *one, two, three* were **chosen by people**. In French they are *un, deux, trois*. In a computer they might be written $1$, $10$, $11$.

Different names. Same numbers.

What is actually real is the **machine** — the fact that there is a starting point and every number has a next one. The names are labels we stuck on afterwards.

<Warning title="Do not say plus one yet">
It is very tempting to say "the next number after $3$ is $3 + 1$."

But look at our two rules. Neither one mentions **adding**! We have not built addition yet. We have zero and next, and that is all.

So for now: the next one after $3$ is *the next one after 3*, which we happen to call $4$. Once addition exists (module 7.6), we will be allowed to say $3+1$ — and then we will have to *prove* the two things agree.
</Warning>

## Why be so fussy?

Because that is the whole point of Chapter 7.

If you sneak in addition before you have built it, you have not really started from the bottom. You have just moved the pile of unexamined assumptions somewhere less visible.

The discipline is: use *only* what the rules give you. It feels slow. It is also the only way to be sure.

<Aside>
Here is something odd to notice. Some books start counting at $0$ and some at $1$. Neither is wrong! It is a choice, like driving on the left or the right.

This course starts at $0$, because that makes the machine cleaner: you get one starting point and one button, and nothing else.
</Aside>
