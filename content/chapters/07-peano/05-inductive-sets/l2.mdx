Module 7.4 gave four rules and there is still a problem. Here it is.

## The rogue model

Take all the counting numbers — $0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ — and then add two extras, $a$ and $b$, with $a$ pointing to $b$ and $b$ pointing back to $a$.

Check the four rules.

Is $0$ there? ✓

Does everything have a next one? ✓ (even $a$ and $b$ do)

Does nothing point at $0$? ✓ ($a$ and $b$ point at each other, not at $0$)

Do different things have different next ones? ✓

**All four rules pass**, and there are two things in there that are clearly not numbers.

## The fix

Call a box **a good box** if:

- $0$ is in it, and
- whenever something is in it, that thing's next one is in it too.

Now here is the last rule:

> **Every good box contains all the counting numbers.**

## Why that kicks out the extras

Make a box and put in $0$, $1$, $2$, $3$, and so on — all the ordinary counting numbers, and *nothing* else. No $a$, no $b$.

Is that a good box? Zero is in it ✓. And the next one after any number in it is also in it ✓.

Yes! It is a good box.

So by the new rule, that box contains all the counting numbers.

But $a$ is not in the box. So **$a$ is not a counting number**. Out it goes, and $b$ with it.

## This is the domino rule again

Look closely at what a good box is.

$0$ is in it — that is *push the first domino*.

Whenever something is in, the next one is in — that is *each domino knocks over the next*.

So a good box is exactly a row of dominoes that all fall! And the rule says: if your box does that, it has everything.

**This is the same rule as Chapter 6.** Induction is not a separate trick. It is the last axiom of arithmetic, wearing different clothes.

<Warning title="Five rules, and now we are done">
Zero exists. Everything has a next. Nothing points at zero. Different things have different nexts. And no extras.

Five rules, and now the counting numbers are pinned down exactly.
</Warning>
